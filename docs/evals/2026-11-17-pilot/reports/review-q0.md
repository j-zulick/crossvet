# The Paris Agreement’s Long-Term Temperature Goal and NDC Update Framework

## Overview

The Paris Agreement sets a collective global temperature objective and establishes a recurring framework for nationally determined contributions (NDCs). Parties must prepare, communicate, and maintain successive NDCs and pursue domestic mitigation measures aimed at achieving them. Each successive contribution is to represent progression beyond the previous contribution and reflect the Party’s highest possible ambition, taking account of responsibilities, capabilities, and national circumstances.

The Agreement also establishes a global stocktake to assess collective progress. Its outcomes inform subsequent NDCs and, more broadly, the updating and enhancement of nationally determined actions and support.

The relevant provisions are Articles 2, 4, and 14. The official text is available in the UNFCCC’s [Paris Agreement PDF](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/Paris_agreement-English.pdf), with explanations on the UNFCCC’s pages on [NDCs](https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/nationally-determined-contributions-ndcs), [Key aspects of the Paris Agreement](https://www.unfccc.int/most-requested/key-aspects-of-the-paris-agreement), and [Global Stocktake](https://unfccc.int/topics/global-stocktake).

## The Paris Agreement’s Long-Term Temperature Goals

### The core temperature objective in Article 2(1)(a)

Article 2(1)(a) states the temperature objective as:

> “Holding the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels...” [1]

The wording contains two connected elements:

- Holding warming **well below 2°C** above pre-industrial levels.
- **Pursuing efforts to limit warming to 1.5°C** above pre-industrial levels.

Both are part of the Agreement’s temperature objective. Describing the goal simply as “2°C” leaves out both “well below” and the explicit reference to efforts toward 1.5°C.

### What the temperature goal means in context

The temperature goal is **collective**: it concerns the increase in global average temperature, rather than establishing a separate temperature limit for each country [1].

The UNFCCC’s explanation describes the temperature goal as the Agreement’s “central aim.” It places that aim alongside increasing countries’ ability to address climate impacts and aligning financial flows with low-emissions, climate-resilient development pathways [3].

The collective temperature objective and individual NDCs therefore operate at different levels. Article 2 states the global direction; Article 4 establishes requirements for Parties’ nationally determined contributions and domestic mitigation efforts.

## How NDCs Work Under Article 4

### What an NDC is

NDCs are the nationally determined contributions through which Parties set out their climate efforts under the Paris Agreement. Their national determination operates within common requirements established by the Agreement.

Article 4(2) provides:

> “Each Party shall prepare, communicate and maintain successive nationally determined contributions that it intends to achieve. Parties shall pursue domestic mitigation measures, with the aim of achieving the objectives of such contributions.” [1]

This establishes duties to:

- **Prepare** NDCs.
- **Communicate** them.
- **Maintain** successive NDCs.
- **Pursue domestic mitigation measures** aimed at achieving their objectives.

The UNFCCC’s NDC page describes NDCs as central to the Agreement and explains these preparation, communication, maintenance, and mitigation requirements [2].

### The five-year submission cycle

Article 4(9) provides:

> “Each Party shall communicate a nationally determined contribution every five years in accordance with decision 1/CP.21 and any relevant decisions of the Conference of the Parties serving as the meeting of the Parties to this Agreement and be informed by the outcomes of the global stocktake referred to in Article 14.” [1]

The provision establishes a **five-year communication obligation**, expressly links it to relevant decisions, and requires it to be informed by global stocktake outcomes. The treaty provision should therefore not be read as a complete, stand-alone account of every submission requirement.

The communication cycle creates recurring occasions for successive NDCs. It does not, by itself, guarantee that their targets will be achieved.

### Progression beyond the previous NDC

Article 4(3) states:

> “Each Party’s successive nationally determined contribution will represent a progression beyond the Party’s then current nationally determined contribution and reflect its highest possible ambition, reflecting its common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, in the light of different national circumstances.” [1]

The progression requirement means that successive NDCs are not intended merely to repeat the current contribution. They are to move beyond it.

This provision establishes an expectation of increasing ambition over time. The quoted text does not prescribe a numerical formula for how much each successive contribution must increase.

### Highest possible ambition in light of national circumstances

Article 4(3) combines **highest possible ambition** with **common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities**, considered in light of different national circumstances [1].

National determination therefore does not mean that the Agreement places no standards on successive contributions. Parties determine their contributions, but Article 4(3) also sets expectations of progression and highest possible ambition.

Nor does the provision demand identical contributions from every Party. Its wording expressly recognizes differences in responsibilities, capabilities, and national circumstances.

### Updating versus achieving

Article 4 distinguishes between **communicating and maintaining an NDC** and **achieving its objectives**.

Article 4(2) requires Parties to prepare, communicate, and maintain successive contributions. It separately requires them to pursue domestic mitigation measures “with the aim of achieving” those contributions’ objectives [1].

The distinction is important: these provisions establish obligations to communicate contributions and pursue measures toward them, rather than guaranteeing that every Party will achieve every target. That does not make the duty to pursue domestic mitigation measures optional.

### Adjustments to existing NDCs

Article 4(11) provides:

> “A Party may at any time adjust its existing nationally determined contribution with a view to enhancing its level of ambition...” [1]

A Party therefore need not wait for the next five-year communication to adjust an existing NDC with a view to increasing ambition. The provision expressly frames such adjustments in terms of **enhancement**.

## The Global Stocktake and Its Role in the NDC Cycle

### What the global stocktake does

Article 14 establishes a periodic assessment of implementation. Article 14(1) states that its purpose is:

> “...to assess the collective progress towards achieving the purpose of this Agreement and its long-term goals...” [1]

The stocktake’s focus is **collective progress**, rather than an assessment of individual countries’ compliance. The UNFCCC’s global stocktake page likewise explains its collective focus [4].

### What the stocktake considers

Article 14(1) specifies:

> “It shall do so in a comprehensive and facilitative manner, considering mitigation, adaptation and the means of implementation and support, and in the light of equity and the best available science.” [1]

The assessment is therefore broader than emissions reductions alone. It considers:

- **Mitigation**.
- **Adaptation**.
- **Means of implementation and support**.

It must also be conducted in light of **equity** and **the best available science**. These are part of the stated basis for assessing collective progress, not separate national targets.

### Timing of the stocktake

Article 14(2) sets the schedule:

> “The Conference of the Parties serving as the meeting of the Parties to this Agreement shall undertake its first global stocktake in 2023 and every five years thereafter...” [1]

The treaty schedule thus identifies 2023 as the first stocktake year, followed by 2028 and subsequent five-year intervals.

Both NDC communication and the stocktake follow five-year cycles. Their connection is expressly established in Article 4(9), which requires NDC communication to be informed by stocktake outcomes [1].

### How the stocktake informs later NDCs

Article 14(3) provides:

> “The outcome of the global stocktake shall inform Parties in updating and enhancing, in a nationally determined manner, their actions and support...” [1]

This language is broader than NDCs alone: it refers to **actions and support**. Article 4(9) supplies the specific link to NDC communication.

Taken together, the provisions establish a recurring relationship:

1. Parties communicate NDCs every five years.
2. Successive NDCs are to represent progression and reflect highest possible ambition.
3. The global stocktake periodically assesses collective progress.
4. Its outcomes inform subsequent NDCs and the updating and enhancement of nationally determined actions and support [1].

The stocktake informs national determination; it does not replace it.

## Key Takeaways

### Temperature goal

Article 2(1)(a) sets a collective objective of holding warming **well below 2°C** above pre-industrial levels and **pursuing efforts to limit warming to 1.5°C**. It does not assign separate country-specific temperature ceilings [1].

### NDC update framework

Article 4 provides that:

- Parties **shall prepare, communicate, and maintain** successive NDCs.
- Parties **shall communicate** an NDC every five years, in accordance with the decisions referenced in Article 4(9), and be informed by stocktake outcomes.
- Each successive NDC **will represent progression** and reflect the Party’s **highest possible ambition**, accounting for responsibilities, capabilities, and national circumstances.
- Parties **shall pursue domestic mitigation measures** aimed at achieving their contributions’ objectives.
- A Party **may adjust** an existing NDC at any time with a view to enhancing ambition [1][2].

These requirements should not be confused with a guarantee that every NDC target will be achieved.

### Role of the global stocktake

Article 14 establishes a comprehensive and facilitative assessment of **collective progress**. It considers mitigation, adaptation, and means of implementation and support, in light of equity and the best available science. Its five-year schedule begins with 2023, and its outcomes inform subsequent NDCs as well as broader nationally determined actions and support [1][4].
